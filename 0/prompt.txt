Committed as `5a7982f`. Summary of what changed in `Physics.java`/`Cast.java`:

**1. Axis-restricted alignment (`dualDerivedNorm`)** — now takes `polNorm` as a 4th argument. Before blending, the incident-angle vector (`rayNorm`) is zeroed down to only its component on `polNorm`'s dominant axis (same largest-abs-component pick `FrustumLod.wouldDoubleCover` already uses). The incident-position term (`octantCenter - incidentPoint`) is left untouched, exactly as you specified. This flows automatically into `dualDerivedAlignment`, so `w`/`commitReflect` and the `Z` blend now ignore off-axis ray drift.

**2. `PolarityIncidentNormal`** — new `Physics.polarityIncidentNormal(polNorm, rayNorm)`. One clarification on how I resolved the ambiguity in "facing toward the ray": a literal whole-vector flip (`dot(N,rayDir)<=0` always) turns out to be self-defeating — it forces `grazeBend`'s and `permeationBend`'s internal sign checks to a constant, destroying the very signal they use to decide *how much* to bend. Instead I implemented it as **per-axis sign-copy** — same idiom `permeationBend` already uses internally — so each component's sign comes from the ray, magnitude comes from `polar`. This guarantees `dot(result, rayNorm) >= 0` always (fixing the 50% backwards-bend case) while keeping both bend functions' existing math and tests completely intact. Applied at all three call sites in `Cast.java` (both `permeationBend` uses and the `grazeBend` use). If you had a different vector in mind (e.g. a purely positional flip against `octantCenter`), flag it — I can swap the implementation, since I only had to guess at the exact math past your description.
